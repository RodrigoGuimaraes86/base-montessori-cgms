From birth to maturity

The Four Planes of Development

Maria Montessori understood human growth as a succession of four stages of six years each, with their own characteristics and needs. In each plane, the child — and later the young person — achieves a new form of independence.

Plane 1 — Infancy (0 to 6 years)

The absorbent mind. This is the period of greatest transformation: the child builds themselves by absorbing the environment. In the first three years (the unconscious absorbent mind), they form language, movement and the foundations of personality; from 3 to 6 (the conscious absorbent mind), they refine and organize what they absorbed through activity and the senses. The central achievement is physical and biological independence: "help me to do it myself".

Plane 2 — Childhood (6 to 12 years)

The reasoning mind and imagination. The child begins to reason, to question and to take interest in how and why things are. A moral sense, a strong social interest and imagination emerge as tools to explore what cannot be seen. This is the stage of Cosmic Education, which presents the universe as an interconnected whole and awakens gratitude and responsibility. The achievement is intellectual independence.

Plane 3 — Adolescence (12 to 18 years)

The "social newborn". The adolescent rebuilds their own identity and seeks their place in society. There is great emotional sensitivity and a need for dignity, belonging and real contribution. Montessori proposed environments in which the young person learns through meaningful work and community life (the idea of the Erdkinder, "children of the land"). The achievement is social and emotional independence.

Plane 4 — Maturity (18 to 24 years)

The construction of the adult. The young adult consolidates their moral and spiritual identity, chooses their path and seeks to contribute to the world. Supported by everything built in the previous planes, they achieve economic independence and full autonomy, becoming an agent of their own life and of society.

“Human development is not uniform and continuous, but takes place through successive rebirths.”

An overview

The planes are not isolated steps: each one prepares the next. What is built in early childhood sustains the reasoning of childhood; intellectual solidity sustains the identity of adolescence; and all of this culminates in an independent adult, conscious of their role in the world. This is why, for Montessori, to educate is to accompany life in each of its phases.
