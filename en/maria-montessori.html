Biography

Maria Montessori

Italian physician, scientist and educator (1870–1952). One of the first women to graduate in medicine in Italy, she devoted her life to understanding the child — and, from that observation, created one of the most influential educational methods in the world.

Maria Montessori was born on 31 August 1870, in the town of Chiaravalle, in the province of Ancona, Italy. She grew up at a time when women's access to higher education — especially the sciences — was rare and met with resistance. Even so, against the expectations of her era, she entered the School of Medicine at the University of Rome, becoming one of the first women physicians in Italy when she graduated in 1896.

From medicine to education

Her early work as a physician brought her into contact with children who had developmental and learning difficulties, then confined to psychiatric institutions. Montessori realized that these children needed, above all, stimulation, activity and a suitable environment — not merely medical care.

She studied deeply the work of the French physicians Jean Itard and Édouard Séguin, pioneers in the education of children with disabilities. Inspired by them, she began developing materials and approaches that respected the child's natural way of learning. The results were remarkable: children considered "uneducable" reached performance comparable to that of others. This led Montessori to a decisive question — if that method worked so well, what could it do for all children?

The first Casa dei Bambini

On 6 January 1907, Montessori had the opportunity to test her ideas with children without disabilities when she opened the first Casa dei Bambini ("Children's House") in the working-class district of San Lorenzo, in Rome. There, observing young children in freedom within a carefully prepared environment, she formulated the principles that would define her method.

“The child is not a vase to be filled, but a spring to be allowed to flow.”

She observed phenomena that impressed her deeply: children's intense concentration on a meaningful task, their spontaneous preference for work over play, their desire for order, and the joy of achieving their own independence. To this natural flourishing she gave the name normalization.

Worldwide spread

In 1909, Montessori published Il Metodo della Pedagogia Scientifica (later known as The Montessori Method), which was quickly translated into many languages. In the following decades she traveled the world lecturing and training educators. Montessori schools appeared across Europe, the Americas and Asia.

In 1929, together with her son Mario Montessori, she founded the Association Montessori Internationale (AMI), created to preserve the integrity of her work and to train teachers. The AMI remains active to this day.

Exile, India and education for peace

With the rise of fascist regimes in Europe, Montessori schools were closed in Italy and Germany during the 1930s, because of their commitment to the child's freedom and autonomy. Montessori left Italy and lived in Spain and later in the Netherlands.

During the Second World War she was in India (1939–1946), where she remained alongside Mario. It was there that she deepened her work with older children and developed the idea of Cosmic Education and her reflection on education as a path to peace — a theme that would mark her final years. For this reason, she was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize three times (1949, 1950 and 1951).

Legacy

Maria Montessori died on 6 May 1952, in Noordwijk aan Zee, in the Netherlands. Her son Mario continued her work. More than a century after the first Casa dei Bambini, there are thousands of Montessori schools around the world, and her principles continue to influence education, developmental psychology and the way we understand childhood.

Timeline

  • 1870
    Born in Chiaravalle, Italy, on 31 August.
  • 1896
    Graduates in medicine from the University of Rome — one of the first women physicians in Italy.
  • 1900
    Becomes co-director of the Orthophrenic School, training teachers and developing educational materials.
  • 1907
    Opens the first Casa dei Bambini in San Lorenzo, Rome.
  • 1909
    Publishes her founding work on "scientific pedagogy".
  • 1929
    Founds the Association Montessori Internationale (AMI) with Mario Montessori.
  • 1939–1946
    Years in India; develops Cosmic Education and education for peace.
  • 1949–1951
    Nominated three times for the Nobel Peace Prize.
  • 1952
    Dies in Noordwijk aan Zee, the Netherlands, on 6 May.
